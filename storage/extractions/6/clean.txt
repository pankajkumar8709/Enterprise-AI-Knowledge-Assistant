# 1. Enterprise Architecture
# 1.1 Platform landscape
NovaCore is the customer-facing application. Its API gateway routes requests to application services. Orion Identity provides authentication and authorization. Atlas Data Lake receives approved analytical events. PulseDesk manages customer cases and operational incidents. LedgerOne manages invoices, payments, purchase approvals, and financial records.
System | Owner | Purpose | Criticality
NovaCore | Engineering | Customer SaaS platform | Critical
Orion Identity | Information Security | Identity and access | Critical
Atlas Data Lake | Data Platform | Analytics and governed data | High
PulseDesk | Customer Operations | Support and incidents | High
LedgerOne | Finance | Billing and financial controls | High
VaultKey | Security Engineering | Secrets management | Critical
DeployHub | Platform Reliability | CI/CD deployment control | Critical
# 1.2 Dependencies
NovaCore depends on Orion Identity for employee administrative authentication.
NovaCore production deployments depend on DeployHub and VaultKey.
Atlas Data Lake receives approved events from NovaCore through the analytics ingestion service.
PulseDesk is the system of record for customer support tickets and incident coordination.
LedgerOne receives billing events from NovaCore through the Finance Integration Service.
# 1.3 Architecture ownership
The Enterprise Architecture Office maintains the authoritative system catalog. Every critical system must have a business owner, technical owner, recovery target, data classification, and documented dependency list. A system cannot enter production without these fields.
# 2. Identity, Access, and Privileged Operations
# 2.1 Access lifecycle
Employees request access through the AccessHub workflow. The employee's manager approves business need, the system owner confirms technical suitability, and Information Security reviews privileged or Restricted-data access. Orion Identity provisions approved access.
Access Type | Approval | Review Frequency
Standard application | Manager | Every 90 days
Production read-only | Manager + System Owner | Every 60 days
Production write | Manager + System Owner + Security | Every 30 days
Privileged administrator | System Owner + Security | Every 30 days
Finance administrator | Finance Owner + Security | Every 30 days
# 2.2 Joiner, mover, leaver rules
New employees must receive a unique corporate identity before access is granted.
Role changes require removal of obsolete privileges before new privileged access is activated.
Involuntary termination access must be disabled within 15 minutes of confirmation.
Voluntary termination access is disabled at the end of the employee's final working period unless Security approves earlier termination.
Contractor accounts must have an expiration date and cannot be indefinite.
# 2.3 Privileged access
Privileged sessions must use MFA, named accounts, and centralized audit logging. Password sharing is prohibited. Break-glass credentials are stored in VaultKey and may be used only during approved emergencies. Break-glass use automatically creates a security review task.
# 3. Data Management and Privacy Operations
# 3.1 Classification
Class | Examples | Handling
Public | Published documentation | Integrity and change control
Internal | Internal procedures | Corporate authentication
Confidential | Contracts, employee records | Encryption + need-to-know
Restricted | Payment data, credentials, security secrets | Encryption + MFA + strict logging
# 3.2 Authoritative datasets
Dataset | System | Business Owner | Retention
Customer tickets | PulseDesk | Customer Operations | 24 months after closure
Analytics events | Atlas Data Lake | Data Platform | 13 months
Billing records | LedgerOne | Finance | 7 years
Security logs | Atlas Data Lake | Information Security | 180 days
Employee records | People systems | People Operations | 7 years after employment ends
# 3.3 Data movement
Confidential and Restricted data may move only through approved encrypted channels. Production data must not be copied into unmanaged development environments. Developers must use synthetic or masked datasets for local development unless a documented exception is approved by the Data Owner and Information Security.
# 3.4 Deletion workflow
Deletion requests are recorded in the Data Governance queue. The Data Owner identifies primary storage, replicas, backups, exports, and downstream datasets. The technical custodian executes deletion according to the approved retention schedule and records evidence. Legal holds override ordinary deletion until released.
# 4. Secure Software Development
# 4.1 Development lifecycle
Engineering teams use Git-based source control, mandatory pull requests, automated testing, dependency scanning, secret scanning, and controlled deployment through DeployHub. Direct production code modification is prohibited except through the emergency procedure.
# 4.2 Required controls
Every production change must reference a ticket or approved change record.
At least one qualified reviewer must approve application-code changes.
Critical security findings must be resolved or formally excepted before release.
Secrets must be stored in VaultKey and must never be committed to source control.
Production configuration changes require peer review and an auditable change record.
# 4.3 Vulnerability targets
Severity | Remediation Target | Exception Authority
Critical | 7 calendar days | CISO
High | 14 calendar days | Security Engineering Lead
Medium | 30 calendar days | System Owner
Low | 90 calendar days | System Owner
# 4.4 Dependency management
Engineering maintains a software bill of materials for NovaCore production releases. Unsupported libraries require an architecture review. A dependency with a known critical vulnerability may not be introduced into production without a CISO-approved exception.
# 5. Change, Release, and Incident Management
# 5.1 Change categories
Category | Approval | Notice
Standard | Pre-approved service owner | No additional notice
Normal | Service owner + reviewer | 4 business hours
High Risk | Engineering owner + Security | 24 hours
Emergency | Incident Commander | Immediate; review next business day
# 5.2 Release procedure
A release candidate must pass automated tests, dependency scanning, configuration validation, and deployment readiness checks. The release owner verifies the rollback plan before production deployment. Platform Reliability monitors the service for at least 30 minutes after a high-risk release.
# 5.3 Incident severity
Severity | Example | Initial Response
S1 | Multi-customer production outage | 15 minutes
S2 | Major feature unavailable | 2 business hours
S3 | Normal defect or degradation | 1 business day
S4 | Question or minor issue | 2 business days
# 5.4 Incident command
For S1 incidents, the Incident Commander owns coordination, the Communications Lead manages stakeholder updates, and the Technical Lead coordinates diagnosis and recovery. Executive notification is required within 30 minutes of confirming an S1 incident. Status updates occur at least every 30 minutes until stabilization.
# 6. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery
# 6.1 Recovery objectives
Service | RPO | RTO | Owner
NovaCore | 24 hours | 4 hours | Platform Reliability
Orion Identity | 1 hour | 2 hours | Information Security
Atlas Data Lake | 24 hours | 8 hours | Data Platform
PulseDesk | 24 hours | 8 hours | Customer Operations
LedgerOne | 24 hours | 8 hours | Finance
# 6.2 Backup policy
Critical production databases receive encrypted daily backups. NovaCore backups are retained for 35 days. Backup restoration is tested quarterly using a representative workload. Restoration evidence must identify the date, dataset, operator, result, and recovery duration.
# 6.3 Disaster recovery exercise
Platform Reliability schedules the quarterly exercise.
System owners validate application dependencies and recovery steps.
Information Security verifies access controls during recovery.
Data Platform validates data integrity after restoration.
Outstanding recovery gaps receive an owner and target date within five business days.
# 6.4 Business continuity
If PulseDesk becomes unavailable, the approved temporary incident channel is the emergency operations mailbox. If Orion Identity becomes unavailable, privileged emergency access may use VaultKey break-glass credentials subject to post-event review.
# 7. Vendor, Procurement, and Third-Party Risk
# 7.1 Vendor onboarding
A business sponsor must create a vendor record before procurement. The record includes vendor name, service, data categories, processing location, business owner, contract status, security assessment status, and renewal date.
Vendor Risk | Required Review | Approval
Low | Procurement review | Business Owner
Medium | Procurement + Security questionnaire | Business Owner + Procurement
High | Security assessment + Legal review | Business Owner + CISO + Legal
Critical | Full risk review + executive acceptance | CISO + General Counsel + Executive Sponsor
# 7.2 Customer-data processors
A processor handling customer Confidential or Restricted information must have an approved contract and security assessment before production access. Production credentials are issued only after the vendor record is marked approved.
# 7.3 Procurement thresholds
Purchases up to INR 100,000 require business-owner approval.
Purchases above INR 100,000 and up to INR 500,000 require Procurement review.
Purchases above INR 500,000 require Finance approval.
Any purchase involving Restricted customer data requires Information Security review regardless of price.
# 8. Customer Operations and Service Management
# 8.1 Support operating model
PulseDesk is the authoritative system for customer cases. Support agents must classify each case, assign a severity, record customer impact, and link related incidents where applicable.
Service Metric | Target | Owner
S1 acknowledgement | 15 minutes | Customer Operations
S2 acknowledgement | 2 business hours | Customer Operations
S3 acknowledgement | 1 business day | Customer Operations
S4 acknowledgement | 2 business days | Customer Operations
S1 status updates | Every 30 minutes | Incident Commander
# 8.2 Customer information handling
Support agents must not request passwords, MFA codes, private keys, or API secrets.
Customer exports must use approved encrypted transfer mechanisms.
Sensitive customer attachments must be stored only in approved PulseDesk locations.
Escalations involving security concerns must be routed to Information Security.
# 8.3 Service review
Customer Operations publishes a monthly service review containing ticket volume, SLA performance, major incidents, recurring issues, and corrective actions. Engineering receives recurring technical-defect trends for prioritization.
# 8.4 Service ownership
The VP Customer Operations is accountable for customer support. Daniel Brooks is the designated executive owner. The NovaCore service owner remains Engineering, while Customer Operations owns the customer-facing support process.
# 9. Governance, Audit, and Knowledge Management
# 9.1 Policy exceptions
Exceptions must document the affected system, policy requirement, business justification, risk, compensating control, owner, approval authority, start date, and expiration date. Security policy exceptions require CISO approval. Exceptions must have an expiration date and cannot be indefinite.
# 9.2 Audit evidence
System owners must retain access-review evidence for at least 12 months.
Change records and deployment evidence must be retained for at least 12 months.
Security incident evidence is retained for at least 180 days unless Legal specifies longer.
Vendor security assessments are retained for the duration of the vendor relationship plus 12 months.
